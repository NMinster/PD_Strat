# Literature review — plasma-proteomic severity and progression in PD (status September 2026)

Purpose: identify (1) work published since the manuscript's reference list was assembled (its newest citations are early-2025 preprints), (2) papers that a referee will expect to see cited or positioned against, and (3) additional analyses the newer literature makes necessary. Full-text access from this environment was limited to search-result abstracts, so page-level details (sample sizes, effect sizes) quoted below should be checked against the papers before citation.

---

## 1. The paper that must be cited first: your own prior study

**Minster & Jafri, *npj Parkinson's Disease* (2026), "Plasma proteomics for Parkinson's disease classification: cross-cohort benchmarking of proteomic, transcriptomic, and multimodal models"** ([journal](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41531-026-01344-5), [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13323761/), [medRxiv v2](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.09.02.25334526v2)).

Same AMP-PD Olink data (1,463 proteins; 971 visit-level samples from 413 participants), matched RNA-seq, **PDBP as development and PPMI as external validation**, proteomic classifier AUROC 0.87 externally, a linear **Proteomic Severity Index (PSI)** explaining 28.2 % of MDS-UPDRS variance with DDC the strongest single severity protein (ρ = 0.61), and a statement that the severity score "predicts future progression".

Consequences for the current manuscript:

1. **Cite it in the Introduction and Methods** and state explicitly what is new: a *continuous severity estimator* as the primary target (not a classifier with a severity side-analysis), full leakage control including HC-anchored normalization and feature selection inside TRAIN, participant-level estimand with bootstrap inference, external transportability of severity, prognostic value beyond baseline UPDRS with replication, and a reduced panel selected without test-set information. Without this paragraph the two papers look like salami slicing.
2. **The cohort roles are reversed** (PDBP→PPMI before; PPMI→PDBP now). A referee will ask why. Give the reason (de novo, medication-naive training cohort → treated, broader-severity test cohort is the harder and more useful direction for a severity model) and, ideally, report the reverse direction as a supplementary sensitivity (the pipeline can do this by swapping `train_prefix`/`test_prefix`).
3. **DDC.** Your prior paper's top severity protein and P20711 in the present locked list — see §3 below; this is now the single most important confound to address.

## 3. Medication confounding — the finding that changes the analysis

**"DOPA-decarboxylase is elevated in CSF, but not plasma, in prodromal and de novo PD"** (2024; [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11165760/)) reports that the *plasma* DDC increase is driven by levodopa/DDC-inhibitor treatment and is absent with dopamine agonists alone. Related: the MDPI 2026 progression paper explicitly flags dopaminergic medication as a potential confounder of the plasma proteome and argues low doses in early PPMI minimise it.

Why this matters here: PPMI (training) is largely untreated at baseline but treated at later visits; PDBP (test) is treated throughout. DDC is P20711 in your locked list, and Fig. 3 shows TEST β ≈ +11 against TRAIN β ≈ +3 — the pattern expected if a treatment-driven protein carries "severity" in the treated cohort. A referee familiar with the 2024 paper will raise this on page one.

Actions (now supported by the pipeline):
- Assembly extracts `on_levodopa`, `on_other_dopaminergic` and `ledd` from `PD_Medical_History.csv` when present; §3i adds them to the covariate set and reports ρ in levodopa-yes / levodopa-no strata.
- `prot_exclude: ["P20711"]` in `config.yaml` re-runs the whole pipeline without DDC (use a separate `out_dir`). Report OOF/TEST ρ with and without DDC, and the DDC coefficient by medication stratum, in Results.
- If ρ within *untreated* rows holds up and the DDC-excluded model transports, the medication objection is answered with data; if not, the manuscript must say that part of the transported signal is pharmacological.

## 4. Context papers reviewers will expect

- **NSD-ISS**: Simuni et al. 2024 (ref 6) is cited; add the performance/validation papers — Dam et al., NSD-ISS performance in PPMI, PASADENA and SPARK baseline cohorts (2024, [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11419206/)) and *Neuronal α-synuclein disease stage progression over 5 years* (Simuni et al., *Mov Disord* 2025, [journal](https://movementdisorders.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/mds.30191)). These define what "anchoring NSD-ISS" would require (functional-impairment stages 3–6 with median times to milestone of 8.3/5.9/2.4 years for stages 2B/3/4); your Cox time-to-H&Y≥3 result is the natural bridge, and the framing should be "a candidate continuous anchor within stages 3–6", not "quantitative anchoring".
- **Quantitative SAA** now exists and is itself a severity/progression marker: Lewy-fold-specific quantitative SAA (Acta Neuropathol 2025, [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11842418/)), endpoint-dilution SD50 (npj PD 2025, [journal](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41531-025-01221-7)), kinetic parameters and progression risk in prodromal PD (*Neurology* 2025, [journal](https://www.neurology.org/doi/10.1212/WNL.0000000000210279)). Your Introduction says "SAA is a binary diagnostic test" — that is no longer accurate; revise to "SAA has been binary in its qualified form; quantitative variants are emerging but require CSF."
- **Neurofilament light** is the incumbent blood progression marker (Mollenhauer et al., *Mov Disord* 2020, [journal](https://movementdisorders.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/mds.28206); EV-NfL 2025, [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12010974/); plasma APLP1 for progression, *Mov Disord* 2025, [journal](https://movementdisorders.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/mds.30154)). NfL is not on the four Olink panels, but reviewers will ask how the proteomic score compares; state this as a limitation and, if PPMI serum NfL is available to you, add it as a comparator in the progression models (one column in `clinical_unified.csv`).
- **GPNMB**: beyond Diaz-Ortiz 2022 (ref 31), cite *Integrative clinical and genomic analyses reveal a causal role of GPNMB in the bone–brain axis of PD* (npj PD 2026, [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13150002/)) and CSF GPNMB–severity association (2024, [journal](https://doi.org/10.1177/1877718x241288712)); plasma GPNMB–MDS-UPDRS II r = 0.35 gives an external replication anchor for your pQTL paragraph.
- **Proteomic organ-age clocks** (Oh/Wyss-Coray lineage; *Nature Aging* 2025 organ-specific clocks, [journal](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43587-025-01016-8); *Nature Medicine* 2025 brain/immune aging, [journal](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-025-03798-1)). Not PD-specific, but "brain age gap" from plasma proteins is a competing continuous readout and a plausible reviewer suggestion ("is your severity score just accelerated brain aging?"). A one-sentence Discussion mention suffices; a comparison would need the clock weights.
- **Proteomic subtypes**: *Defining proteomic subtypes to understand the heterogeneity of PD* ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12737882/)), *Longitudinal proteomic profiling defines robust molecular subtypes* (bioRxiv Nov 2025), *Data-driven biological subtypes of PD* (medRxiv 2026, [preprint](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.07.28.26359072v1.full)), *One patient, one destiny* PPMI cluster analysis ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13049996/)). If you keep any subtyping, these are the comparators; given your K = 2 fallback with silhouette ≈ 0, the cleaner choice is to cite them and state that stable proteomic subtypes were not detected in this feature space.
- **Cross-cohort Olink harmonisation**: SPOC synthetic-pool correction ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11653412/)) and the serum/plasma comparison method (*MCP* 2025, [journal](https://www.mcponline.org/article/S1535-9476(25)00099-4/fulltext)). Cite when describing HC-anchored normalisation vs within-cohort z-scoring (§10l-iv), and state whether AMP-PD ran PPMI and PDBP on shared plates with bridging samples.
- **Model-validation standards**: TRIPOD+AI (Collins et al., *BMJ* 2024) should be cited and the checklist supplied; the *npj PD* 2025 review of ML for PD ([journal](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41531-025-01025-9)) documents the external-validation gap you argue from and replaces the weaker "[13]" citation.
